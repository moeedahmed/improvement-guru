# Measures and run charts

## The family of measures

- **Outcome measure:** the result that matters to patients or staff. It is often the aim measure.
- **Process measure:** whether the steps that should lead to the outcome are happening.
- **Balancing measure:** something that could get worse because of the change, such as workload, delays elsewhere or a safety check being skipped.

## Operational definitions

Write each definition so that two people would count the same way:

- what is counted or timed, from when to when;
- the numerator and denominator;
- inclusions and exclusions;
- the type of data source, in general terms (for example "the department's electronic record" or "a paper tally sheet");
- how often it is collected (weekly is a good default) and which role collects it.

Collect aggregate figures only, such as weekly counts or a weekly median time. A QI measure rarely needs anything that identifies a person, and individual times on a named shift can identify people.

## Run charts

- Plot one measure over time, in time order, with the median of the baseline points drawn as a line.
- Aim for at least 10 baseline points before judging signals. With fewer, treat any signal as provisional.
- Mark the date each PDSA change started.

### Signals

These are the rules used by the Improvement Guru toolkit:

- **Shift:** 6 or more consecutive points all above, or all below, the baseline median. In this toolkit a point exactly on the median resets the count. Some guides skip such points instead, so say which rule was used.
- **Trend:** 5 or more consecutive points, each strictly higher or each strictly lower than the one before. Two equal points in a row break the trend.
- The baseline median is not recalculated after a shift unless the team agrees the process has changed.
- A signal suggests the change is not random variation. It does not prove the PDSA change caused it, so check what else changed at the same time.

For statistical process control (SPC) charts, see the HQIP guide and the NHS IMPACT resources in `uk-sources.md`.
