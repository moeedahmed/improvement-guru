# QI starter pack: Faster first pain relief at Riverside ED

Status: draft for learning and planning. All figures are fictional. Confirm everything locally.

## 1. Project charter

- Project title: Faster first pain relief for adults in severe pain
- Setting (general terms): Riverside Emergency Department (fictional), adult areas.
- Problem statement: Adults who arrive in severe pain wait a median of 58 minutes (fictional baseline, 8 weeks) from arrival to their first analgesia. Staff report that pain scores are not always recorded at triage, so severe pain is not always visible to the staff who can give or prescribe relief.
- Why it matters: patient experience and timeliness. Untreated pain causes distress and can make assessment harder.
- In scope: adults (as defined by the department [to confirm locally]) recorded at triage with severe pain on the department's pain scale; first analgesia only.
- Out of scope for this cycle: children; the choice of analgesic, dose or route, which are clinical decisions for local guidelines; pain reassessment, which could be a later cycle.
- Team roles needed: emergency medicine consultant lead, triage nurse lead, nurse in charge, a data or digital contact, a patient representative.
- Risks and safeguards: extra triage workload (watched through the balancing measure); triage being rushed (balancing measure); no identifiable data collected (weekly aggregate counts and medians only).

## 2. SMART aim

Aim: By 31 May 2027, we aim to reduce the median time from arrival to first analgesia from 58 minutes to 30 minutes (fictional local target) for adults triaged with severe pain at Riverside ED, while monitoring the time from arrival to triage.

| Check | Pass or fix | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Specific | Pass | One measure, one patient group, one department. |
| Measurable | Pass | Baseline of 58 minutes and target of 30 minutes, both fictional. |
| Achievable | Fix | Halving the median in six months is ambitious, so add an interim target of 45 minutes after the first cycles. |
| Relevant | Pass | Timeliness and patient experience. |
| Time-bound | Pass | 31 May 2027. |

Revised aim: By 31 May 2027, we aim to reduce the median time from arrival to first analgesia from 58 minutes to 30 minutes, with an interim target of 45 minutes by 28 February 2027 (both fictional local targets), for adults triaged with severe pain at Riverside ED, while monitoring the time from arrival to triage.

## 3. Measures

| Type | Measure | Operational definition | Numerator and denominator, or timing | Frequency | Data source (general) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Outcome | Median time from arrival to first analgesia | Adults triaged with severe pain; excludes patients who decline analgesia [to confirm locally] | From the arrival time to the first recorded analgesia time; median for the week | Weekly | The department's electronic record |
| Process | Pain score recorded at triage | Adult triage records that include a pain score | Numerator: adult triage records with a pain score. Denominator: all adult triage records that week | Weekly | The department's electronic record |
| Balancing | Median time from arrival to triage | All adult arrivals | From the arrival time to the start of triage; median for the week | Weekly | The department's electronic record |

Here the aim measure is used as the outcome measure. A patient-reported measure, such as pain reassessment, could be added in a later cycle.

How we will chart it: a weekly run chart for each measure, with the baseline median drawn (58 minutes for the outcome measure) and the start of each PDSA test marked. There are 8 fictional baseline points so far, so any early signal is provisional until there are at least 10. A shift would be 6 or more weeks in a row below 58 minutes. A trend would be 5 or more weeks in a row, each lower than the one before.

## 4. First PDSA test

Change ideas considered:

1. Make the pain score a required field at triage (driver: pain is not always recorded).
2. Add a severe-pain flag to the department's patient board so the nurse in charge can see it (driver: severe pain is not visible).
3. Put a prompt card at triage that points to the department's existing analgesia guideline (driver: uncertainty about the local pathway). This needs the guideline owner's agreement before any test.
4. Add a short reminder to the start-of-shift huddle (driver: awareness).

Chosen change and why: idea 2. It is small, needs no system change, is easy to stop and can be tried on one shift.

| Step | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Plan | The triage nurse adds a severe-pain flag to the board entry for each adult triaged with severe pain, and the nurse in charge reviews flagged patients on the board at an interval agreed locally [to confirm locally]. One triage nurse and the nurse in charge, adult area, one weekday day shift [date to confirm locally]. Prediction: at least 8 of the next 10 flagged patients get first analgesia within 30 minutes of arrival. Data: a tally sheet for the shift that counts how many flagged patients got first analgesia within 30 minutes of arrival and how many did not, with no names, numbers or individual times. The time to triage for the shift comes from the department's routine report. Stop rule: stop if staff report that triage is being delayed, or raise a safety or workload concern. |
| Do | Record what happened, any changes to the plan and staff comments. |
| Study | Compare the results with the prediction, check the time to triage on that shift, and ask both staff members what helped and what got in the way. |
| Act | Adopt (try it on more shifts) if 8 or more of 10 get analgesia within 30 minutes with no delay to triage. Adapt (change who checks, or how often) if 5 to 7 do. Abandon (test another idea) if fewer than 5 do, or if triage is delayed. |
