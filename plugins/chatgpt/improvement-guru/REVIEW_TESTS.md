## 3. Clinic letters to GPs take ages at our outpatient clinic. How would I turn that into a QIP?

**Expected answer.** Charter: improve the turnaround for routine outpatient letters. SMART aim: in this fictional clinic, raise the proportion sent within seven days from 40% to 80% by 31 May 2027. Specific, measurable and time-bound pass. Confirm achievability with the fictional team and link relevance to timely handover. Measures: percentage sent on time (letters on time divided by letters due), percentage assigned to a sign-off queue (assigned divided by drafted), and median signing time. Use made-up weekly figures only. PDSA: trial one sign-off slot for ten fictional letters. Predict eight on time. Adopt for a larger test if eight or more are on time without added delay, adapt for five to seven, and abandon below five or if signing delays increase. Chart weekly results against the fictional baseline. Use the IHI Model for Improvement and leave registration, governance, data access and patient involvement to confirm locally.

**Text check.** Stages 0–5, the clinic-letters scenario and the sources list. Append the standard Limits for the full pack.

## 4. Is this a good SMART aim: improve sepsis screening by March?

**Expected answer.** This needs more detail. Specific: fix by naming the setting and eligible records. Measurable: fix by adding a baseline and target. Achievable: check capacity. A fictional example: by 31 March 2027, increase the proportion of eligible adult ED records with documented sepsis screening from 64% to 90%, while monitoring median time to triage. Eligibility follows the fictional department's criteria.

**Text check.** Stage 2 and the SMART rubric support the check table and revised aim. Scenario B supplies fictional figures. Ground rule 4 keeps clinical criteria outside the reply.
